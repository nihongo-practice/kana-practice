English letters are a guide to the sounds. を / ヲ is usually pronounced “o” and is often typed “wo”. は and へ sound like “wa” and “e” when used as particles. This chart shows the 46 basic characters; small kana and characters with marks are a later step.

Mixing up similar characters?
シ shi · ツ tsu

In シ, the two short strokes sit down the left side. In ツ, they sit across the top.

ン n · ソ so

For ン, write the long stroke from the lower left toward the upper right. For ソ, write it from the upper right toward the lower left.

ぬ nu · め me

ぬ has a small loop at the lower right. め does not.

ね ne · れ re

ね ends in a loop on the right. れ does not.

Printed characters can look different from handwriting. For writing practice, also check your teacher’s example.

Opening the file & using sound

Save kana-practice.html to your device and open it in a web browser. Use the browser, rather than an email or messaging app’s attachment preview. Some mobile preview apps do not run quizzes.

Learning and practice work offline. Listen says each sound once at a slower pace, using your device’s Japanese voice if available. Let the sound finish before playing another character. Voice availability and internet use depend on your device. If sound is unavailable, you can still use every practice question.

Your practice stays in this browser on this device. It is not included when you send this file to someone else.
